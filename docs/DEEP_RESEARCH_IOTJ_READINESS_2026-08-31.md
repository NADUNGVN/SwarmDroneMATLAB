**Date:** 31 August 2026  
**Decision:** IoT-J is the nearest target, but the study is not yet submission-ready.

## Bottom line

The direction is good and fits IEEE Internet of Things Journal. The journal's published scope includes IoT networking/protocols, constrained systems, applications, testbeds and trials; its AoI call explicitly includes data semantics, sensing–communication–control co-design and proof-of-concept evaluation ([IoT-J author guidelines](https://ieee-iotj.org/guidelines-for-authors/); [official AoI call](https://ieee-iotj.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/CFP_AoI_IoTJ.pdf)). Hardware is not universally mandatory: analytical and simulation-centered work is publishable when its theoretical or algorithmic result is sufficiently strong ([official IoT-J issue digest](https://www.comsoc.org/system/files/2021-10/Publications_Contents_Digest_2021_Oct.pdf)).

For this manuscript, however, a measured radio bridge is the shortest credible route. The present theory explains the mechanism but is not strong enough to replace empirical network validation, while the selected policy is supported over only three confirmatory cells and its CSMA RMSE gains are modest (0.97–1.22%).

## What is already strong

- Clear causal separation between true receiver freshness and sender-estimated/confirmed freshness.
- Preregistration, paired seeds, confidence intervals, Holm correction, frozen artifacts and explicit negative results.
- Multiple communication-cost definitions rather than a single favorable accounting convention.
- Branch-at-decision replay showing that confirmation lead is not automatically receiver/control value.
- EXP14I succeeds in all 6/6 Holm-controlled directional tests across 3/3 registered cells. The ALOHA reversal is material: 11.84% lower RMSE and 4.51% lower offered utilization than piggyback-only.
- EXP15 already uses the correct counterfactual contract: policy-independent absolute-time calibration, not replay of a trace generated by one tested policy.

This is a strong scientific foundation. The remaining problems are novelty positioning, breadth and communication realism—not basic experimental discipline.

## Where the current novelty collides with prior work

The paper cannot safely claim that the novelty is ACK-aware event triggering. ACK/no-ACK event-triggered control and receiver-specific acknowledgement mechanisms already exist ([Dolk and Heemels, Automatica 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2017.02.029); [Garcia, Cao, and Casbeer, IET CTA 2016](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/iet-cta.2016.0107); [Viel et al., Automatica 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2022.110215)).

It also cannot claim delayed-ACK belief as new. Random two-way delay, delayed-feedback AoI and belief-based partial observability are established ([Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02929); [Wang 2024](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ecetr/764/); [Tahir et al., IFIP Networking 2024](https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/networking/networking2024/1570998329.pdf)).

Nor is the generic statement “MAC changes the AoI conclusion” sufficient. AoI under ALOHA/CSMA and a MAC perspective for IoT control have already been developed ([Kadota et al., INFOCOM 2020](https://www.mit.edu/~kadota/PDFs/INFOCOM_2020.pdf); [Mena and Núñez, Automatica 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2022.110652)). Piggyback-assisted AoI-aware MAC design also exists ([Peng et al., IEEE TWC 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10242)).

The most important collision is Munari and Badia: they directly analyze when feedback is worth its transmission cost and identify regimes where feedback or no feedback is preferable ([IEEE TCOM 2025 record](https://elib.dlr.de/216423/); [GLOBECOM full text](https://www.dei.unipd.it/~badia/papers/2022_12_Globecom2022151866.pdf)). Therefore, the present title/question “when standalone ACKs help” is too broad to be the main novelty.

## The contribution that remains defensible

The targeted search did not locate prior work that directly demonstrates this entire causal chain in shared-medium semantic broadcast control:

> a standalone ACK changes the transmitter's belief about receiver state; that belief changes later DATA decisions; those suppressed or redirected DATA transmissions determine actual receiver freshness and closed-loop performance; therefore earlier confirmation can have zero or negative downstream value, and the preferred feedback route can reverse over a declared access/load envelope.

That is a plausible and useful research gap. It must be presented as a qualified integrated contribution—“we jointly expose and quantify”—rather than an unqualified priority claim. A final backward/forward citation audit is still needed immediately before submission.

## Mandatory work before submission

| Priority | Work package | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Repair literature and novelty | Add ACK-aware ETC, delayed-feedback AoI, MAC-perspective, feedback-cost and WiSwarm streams; explicitly state that individual ACK/piggyback/AoI/MAC ingredients are not new |
| P0 | Add closest practical baseline | Implement one ACK-aware ETC/receiver-specific ACK comparator in addition to the existing delayed-belief, periodic, state-trigger and route baselines |
| P1 | EXP16 factorial holdout | Preregister feedback route × access interaction and test difference-in-differences for RMSE and offered airtime using matched conditions/shared seeds |
| P1 | Operating-envelope map | At least two loads, two loss/burst levels, N=5/N=10 and a reverse-asymmetry or hidden-terminal condition; report stable, reversal and indeterminate regions |
| P1 | Real sensitivity analysis | Freeze policy; enlarge beyond the current 12-point/3-seed LHS; vary ACK delay/deferral, age/refresh thresholds, access scaling, frame size, load and burstiness |
| P1 | EXP15 measured trace | Separate policy-independent DATA/ACK loss and background occupancy; validate abstraction assumptions |
| P1 | Live CSMA bench | Randomized crossover on 3–5 nodes over days/geometries; synchronized packet capture and application logs |
| P2 | Energy/runtime | Two-node measured radio energy plus timing/memory complexity on the companion computer |
| P2 | Reproducibility | One-command reproduction, generated figures/tables, environment capture, public frozen tag and archival DOI |
| P2 | Threat model | Honest but delayed/lost/duplicated/reordered ACKs; authenticated-frame/MAC discussion; no security claim unless tested |

The current EXP14I directional tests are good evidence that each selected route wins in its registered cell, but they are not a formal test of a route-by-MAC interaction. EXP16 should make that interaction the primary estimand instead of inferring it from separate one-sided tests.

## Hardware boundary

The default Raspberry Pi 5 + AR9271/ath9k_htc Linux/mac80211 plan is a reasonable low-cost CSMA testbed; official Linux documentation supports the adapter and IBSS/monitor modes ([Linux Wireless documentation](https://wireless.docs.kernel.org/en/latest/en/users/drivers/ath9k_htc.html)). It should be described as a measured 802.11 layer, not equated with the current abstract p-persistent CSMA model.

A Wi-Fi application cannot produce genuine ALOHA while the underlying 802.11 MAC still performs carrier sensing. Recommended scope:

- experimentally validate the CSMA branch on Wi-Fi;
- keep ALOHA as a clearly labeled simulation boundary;
- add SDR/controllable-radio hardware only if real cross-MAC validation becomes a central claim.

No final UAV platform or flight campaign is needed for the nearest submission. WiSwarm shows the higher systems benchmark ([IEEE INFOCOM 2023](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10228860/)), but a credible 3–5-node radio bench plus trace replay and energy measurement is enough to establish the networking bridge. Flight can be a later extension.

## Go/no-go rule

Proceed toward IoT-J only if all of the following hold without post-hoc retuning:

1. the factorial feedback-route × access interaction survives multiplicity control;
2. its sign is stable over a declared nontrivial operating envelope, with failures disclosed;
3. measured-trace replay preserves the qualitative mechanism;
4. the live 802.11 bench validates the CSMA-side conclusion and measured cost/energy accounting;
5. the paper's novelty is narrowed to causal feedback mediation and no longer depends on claiming that ACK-aware triggering, piggybacking or MAC-dependent AoI is new.

If the measured layer does not preserve the mechanism, the result is still scientifically valuable, but the paper must be reframed as an idealized-channel boundary study rather than a networked IoT solution.

## Recommended execution order

1. Rewrite only the literature map, novelty ledger and baseline specification.
2. Freeze/preregister EXP16 and the sensitivity study before inspecting new performance results.
3. Run EXP16 and robustness mapping.
4. Execute EXP15 calibration and the 3–5-node CSMA bench.
5. Add energy/runtime, public artifact and threat-model section.
6. Rewrite the full manuscript and conduct a fresh IoT-J-style internal review.
