\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Periodic communication is predictable, but its rate is fixed before a
mission whose network quality may be unknown or changing. Event-triggered
communication can react to state evolution, yet a sender does not directly
observe what a receiver has received when DATA and feedback are delayed or
lost. This distinction matters in multi-UAV formation control: repeated
in-flight packets can consume communication without refreshing the receiver,
whereas excessive suppression can hide genuinely new state information.

We study one fixed causal policy that changes its communication effort as
network quality changes without receiving a regime label. The policy uses
cumulative acknowledgements (ACKs) to maintain an estimate of receiver
freshness, separates the last state sent from the last state confirmed, and
distinguishes new-information traffic from refresh traffic. Freshness affects
the event threshold, while an in-flight cooldown suppresses only redundant
refreshes. All decisions use locally available state, sampled time, and causal
feedback.

The primary contribution is causal within-mission communication adaptivity
under unknown network conditions, rather than universal Pareto dominance over
fixed periodic communication. The evidence is deliberately separated into a
mechanistic claim and mission-level trade-offs. In a preregistered 50-seed
holdout, the fixed Causal-v3 policy changes DATA effort across network regimes
and improves Stressed accuracy relative to P10. ACK-inclusive accounting,
however, reverses the apparent Stressed communication advantage. In the
changing-network EXP11 mission, Causal-v3 adapts its rate within a run and has
lower ACK-weighted cost than P20 at the preregistered $w=0.25$, while P12.5
retains \MetricElevenPonetwofiveAccuracyPercent{}\% of its accuracy at
\MetricElevenPonetwofiveCostPercent{}\% of its $\mathrm{Total}_{w025}$ cost and
\MetricElevenPonetwofiveBroadcastPercent{}\% of its broadcast cost.

The paper makes three contributions. First, it gives an implementable causal
ACK-assisted AoI trigger with dual memory and an explicit innovation/refresh
semantic split. Second, a frozen v1--v3 ablation isolates redundant in-flight
retransmission and the over-suppression failure that motivated that split.
Third, frozen simulation experiments report nominal, changing-network,
communication-cost, robustness-boundary, and 6-DOF evidence with matched-seed
confidence intervals and explicit negative results.

The claims do not include universal superiority over a tuned periodic rate,
general ACK-inclusive or broadcast-medium traffic reduction, hardware
validation, or a closed-loop stability guarantee. These boundaries are part
of the main results rather than deferred to supplementary material.
