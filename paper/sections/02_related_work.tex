\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Status of this section.} This draft contains \emph{no

What follows is therefore the \emph{argument structure} the section will

\subsection{Age of information in networked control}

AoI originated as a freshness metric for status-update systems, where the
question is how stale a monitor's information is rather than how much
throughput a link carries. Its migration into control is the relevant
thread: minimising average age is not the same objective as minimising
control error, and a policy that optimises age alone can transmit when
nothing has changed.

\emph{What we need from this group:} that age-based scheduling is
established as distinct from rate- or throughput-based scheduling, and that
the age-versus-control-cost mismatch is a known issue rather than our
observation.

\emph{Where we differ:} age never triggers a transmission in our policy.
It modulates the state-change threshold that does. The composition is
multiplicative on the threshold, not additive on the decision.

\subsection{Event-triggered and self-triggered multi-agent control}

Event-triggered control replaces periodic sampling with a
condition on local error, and the multi-agent literature extends this to
consensus and formation problems, typically with guarantees that exclude
Zeno behaviour.

\emph{What we need from this group:} the standard state-error trigger form
we use as a baseline, and the accepted treatment of a minimum inter-event
time.

\emph{Where we differ:} our trigger reads an estimate of what the
\emph{receiver} holds, which a purely local error trigger cannot access.
The comparison in this paper is against precisely that conventional local
trigger, and the gap is large (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{AoI-aware and event-triggered wireless control}

The closest body of work combines freshness with triggering. Two
assumptions recur and both matter to us: that the sender knows the
receiver's age exactly, and that acknowledgements are free or ideal.

\emph{What we need from this group:} evidence that the oracle-freshness
assumption is common, so that removing it is a contribution rather than a
self-imposed handicap.

\emph{Where we differ:} we remove it, and we measure the cost. Our frozen
ablation includes an acausal oracle variant as an explicit upper reference,
and the comparison is more interesting than expected: the causal policy
reaches \emph{lower} Stressed error than the oracle
(\MetricSevenAfivecStressedRmse{}~m against
\MetricSevenIdealStressedRmse{}~m) by transmitting more
(\MetricSevenAfivecStressedRate{}~Hz against
\MetricSevenIdealStressedRate{}~Hz per channel). The oracle is not a
performance ceiling; it is an \emph{efficiency} ceiling, and treating it as
the former overstates what free freshness information buys.

\subsection{UAV swarm communication constraints}

Field-relevant constraints --- limited airtime, shared medium, duty-cycle
and energy budgets, degradation with swarm size --- motivate why trading
formation accuracy for fewer transmissions is worth doing at all.

\emph{What we need from this group:} realistic airtime and duty-cycle
figures for the radios such a swarm would use, and evidence on how neighbour
count limits sustainable rates.

\emph{Where we differ, and where we are weak:} we price communication with
three packet-count cost models, an airtime proxy and a broadcast proxy, but
we measure no physical airtime or energy. This is stated as a limitation
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) and is the first thing the hardware
roadmap addresses.

\subsection{Acknowledgement- and feedback-aware scheduling}

Protocol-level work on ACK-aware scheduling, cumulative acknowledgement and
retransmission logic is directly relevant, because our reverse channel is
not an abstraction: it has delay, jitter and loss, and its traffic is
priced.

\emph{What we need from this group:} standard cumulative-ACK semantics, and
prior treatment of the cost of the reverse channel in control-oriented
scheduling.

\emph{Where we differ:} our loss recovery introduces no retransmission
timeout at all. An unacknowledged packet remains outstanding, outstanding
packets forbid refresh, and the max-silence backstop then fires. Whether
that semantic correction alone suffices was the question the third design
iteration existed to answer.

\subsection{Resource-aware formation control and co-design}

Finally, the co-design view: communication and control designed together
rather than layered. Our results speak to a specific asymmetry within it.
Several of our rejected claims are \emph{control} limitations that no
communication policy could remove --- a steady offset under mass mismatch,
safety failures shared identically across all four methods under link and
node faults.

\emph{What we need from this group:} the framing that separates
communication-attributable from controller-attributable degradation.

\emph{Where we differ:} we attribute explicitly, and in the cases above the
attribution is not to our contribution.
