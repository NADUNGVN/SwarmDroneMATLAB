\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Event-triggered multi-agent control}
Event-triggered control replaces periodic sampling by state-dependent
decisions~\cite{astrom2002riemann,tabuada2007event,heemels2012introduction}.
Distributed extensions establish consensus and coordination under sampled or
dynamic triggers~\cite{dimarogonas2012distributed,seyboth2013eventbased,girard2015dynamic,yi2017distributed,nowzari2019eventtriggered},
with later work addressing communication frequency and dynamic or learned
triggers~\cite{chen2020howoften,ge2021dynamic,zhang2025overview,li2026impulsive}.
Multi-agent UAV studies likewise use event-based formation or containment
updates~\cite{yin2023eventbased,ji2023dynamic,chen2024distributed,yang2025fencing}.
Kesper \emph{et al.} maintain age/timer information for last broadcast states
in distributed multi-agent event-triggered control~\cite{kesper2023toward}, but
do not use delayed cumulative ACKs to reconstruct confirmed receiver state.

\subsection{AoI in networked control}
Age of information (AoI) measures time since the generation of the newest
received update~\cite{kaul2012realtime,sun2017update}; surveys and refinements
cover scheduling, gossip, value of information, and task relevance
~\cite{yates2021aoisurvey,yang2025aoiperspective,kaswan2025gossip,ayan2019aoivoi,rajaraman2021notjustage}.
Mamduhi \emph{et al.} combine freshness and event-based scheduling in
multi-loop networked control~\cite{mamduhi2020freshness}; Wang \emph{et al.}
study freshness-aware control~\cite{wang2021freshness}; and Lin \emph{et al.}
provide an AoI-aware event-triggered load-frequency-control precedent
~\cite{lin2023eventtriggered}. These works motivate freshness-sensitive
decisions but do not reproduce our sender-side confirmed-state mechanism.

\subsection{ACK and delayed-feedback AoI}
ACK/NACK-driven causal AoI scheduling is explicit in Ceran \emph{et al.}
~\cite{ceran2019average}; related scheduling includes Whittle-index and
wireless-control formulations~\cite{tang2022whittle,gatsis2014optimal,park2018wireless}.
Tahir \emph{et al.} are a close decentralized delayed-ACK neighbor that tracks
a belief about receiver AoI~\cite{tahir2024collaborative}. Onozuka \emph{et
al.} apply AoI event triggering to forward and feedback railway-control paths
~\cite{onozuka2024aoi}. Classical networked-control results establish the
importance of delays, loss, and feedback information
~\cite{walsh2002stability,hespanha2007survey,tatikonda2004control,nair2007feedback,molin2013optimality,ramesh2013design,demirel2017tradeoff}.

\subsection{UAV and resource-aware communication}
UAV-network surveys emphasize constrained, mobile wireless links
~\cite{gupta2016survey,zeng2016wireless,mozaffari2019tutorial,zeng2019accessing,campion2019uavswarm}.
AoI-aware UAV networking and WiSwarm connect freshness to aerial networking
and collaborative flight~\cite{amodu2023aoiuav,tripathi2023wiswarm}. Formation
control and swarm demonstrations provide the control context
~\cite{olfatisaber2006flocking,oh2015survey,lee2010geometric,mellinger2011minimum,vasarhelyi2018optimized,zhou2022swarm}.

\subsection{Nearest-neighbor gap}
The closest strands are therefore Mamduhi's AoI/event scheduling, Ceran's
causal ACK scheduling, WiSwarm's AoI-based UAV networking, Tahir's delayed-ACK
receiver-AoI belief, Kesper's distributed broadcast-age state, Onozuka's
bidirectional AoI triggering, and Lin's AoI-aware event trigger. We did not
identify prior work combining delayed cumulative ACKs, an ACK-confirmed
receiver-state estimate, separate \emph{lastSent}/\emph{lastAcked} memories,
state innovation, and in-flight refresh suppression in one multi-UAV
formation policy. This is a scoped search conclusion, not a priority claim;
the audited comparison is retained in the public related-work matrix.
