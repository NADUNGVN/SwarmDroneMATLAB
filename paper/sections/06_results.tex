\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig03_design_evolution}
\caption{The causal chain in the accuracy-versus-traffic plane, Stressed
condition. The proposed policy reaches lower error than the non-causal
ideal-feedback reference by transmitting more. The latter is an efficiency
reference, not a bound on attainable accuracy.}
\label{fig:evolution}
\end{figure}

Four observations, each of which the chain isolates.

\textbf{A fixed threshold does not adapt.} The open-loop, non-adaptive
variant transmits \MetricSevenAtwocCleanRate{},
\MetricSevenAtwocModerateRate{} and \MetricSevenAtwocStressedRate{}~Hz
across Clean, Moderate and Stressed --- essentially flat --- for a Stressed
error of \MetricSevenAtwocStressedRmse{}~m, worse than P10.

\textbf{Adaptation on an open loop adapts the wrong way.} Adding the
freshness-adaptive threshold while still estimating freshness from what the
sender transmitted gives \MetricSevenAthreecCleanRate{},
\MetricSevenAthreecModerateRate{} and \MetricSevenAthreecStressedRate{}~Hz.
The rate \emph{decreases} as the network degrades. The mechanism is direct:
an open-loop sender believes the receiver is as fresh as its own last
attempt, so it becomes \emph{less} willing to transmit exactly when
packets are being lost.

\textbf{Closing the loop is not sufficient on its own.} With freshness
taken from acknowledgements but new information and refresh still
conflated, the rate is \MetricSevenAfourcCleanRate{},
\MetricSevenAfourcModerateRate{} and \MetricSevenAfourcStressedRate{}~Hz:
the Moderate and Stressed values are pinned to the same figure. The
refresh cooldown has become a ceiling on \emph{new information}, so the
policy cannot respond to a worse network at all. This is the failure
mode of our second design iteration.

\textbf{Separating the two semantics resolves it, with no parameter
change.} The proposed policy transmits \MetricSevenAfivecCleanRate{},
\MetricSevenAfivecModerateRate{} and \MetricSevenAfivecStressedRate{}~Hz
for a Stressed error of \MetricSevenAfivecStressedRmse{}~m. Every
parameter is identical to the pinned variant above.

A result we did not anticipate is worth stating plainly. The non-causal
ideal-feedback reference --- which reads receiver state directly in the
same timestep ---
runs at \MetricSevenIdealStressedRate{}~Hz for
\MetricSevenIdealStressedRmse{}~m. The causal policy is \emph{more
accurate} (\MetricSevenAfivecStressedRmse{}~m) while transmitting
\emph{more} (\MetricSevenAfivecStressedRate{}~Hz). Free freshness
information does not buy accuracy; it buys the ability to be quiet. It
therefore characterizes the cost of causal information, rather than
bounding achievable accuracy, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} takes this
up.

Two features of this table are unfavourable to our method and are the

\scriptsize
\input{tables/tableV_robustness}

Table~\ref{tab:robust} summarises the robustness axes and
Figures~\ref{fig:ackimp}--\ref{fig:mismatch} give the detail.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig06_ack_impairment}
\caption{Reverse-channel impairment. Left, Moderate: increasing ACK loss
and delay drives the adaptive scale down to its floor and the transmission
rate up. Right, Stressed: the scale is \emph{already} at the floor, so the
response is flat. The flatness is saturation of the adaptation mechanism,
not robustness to impairment.}
\label{fig:ackimp}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{ACK impairment.} In the Stressed condition, error and
transmission rate are unchanged between a reliable reverse channel and one
with $20\%$ loss and forward-equivalent delay
(\MetricAckImpStressedReliableRmse{}~m against
\MetricAckImpStressedImpairedRmse{}~m). This must not be read as
robustness. The adaptive scale is pinned at its floor
($s_{\min}$, observed mean \MetricAckImpStressedImpairedScale{}) in both
cases, so the mechanism that would respond to reverse-channel degradation
has no remaining range. The Moderate panel of Figure~\ref{fig:ackimp}
shows the mechanism working before it saturates: there the same impairment
moves the scale to its floor and raises the rate.

\paragraph{Topology and swarm size.} Across four topologies and
$N \in \{10,20,50\}$ the communication ranking is preserved
(Figure~\ref{fig:topology}), but absolute safety generalisation was
rejected: the safety gate fails systematically at one condition of the
sweep, and a follow-up diagnostic attributed it to a consensus gain that is
not normalised by in-degree --- a controller property, not a
communication one.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig07_topology}
\caption{Formation error against mean consensus in-degree across four
topologies and three swarm sizes. The ordering between methods is stable;
the absolute level is not, and the trend with in-degree is a controller
effect.}
\label{fig:topology}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Link and node faults.} Under $20\%$ permanent directed-link
removal and under a $5$~s single-node communication blackout, safety fails
--- and fails at the \emph{same rate for every method}. In the holdout
matrix the unsafe fraction under permanent link removal is identical for
P10, P20 and the proposed policy. A failure shared identically by a
$20$~Hz periodic baseline and an event-triggered policy is not a property
of the communication policy. Figure~\ref{fig:fault} shows the traffic
response and recovery.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig08_fault_response}
\caption{Node-blackout response. Left: DATA rate before, during and after
a $5$~s blackout. Right: time to return to the pre-fault error band.
Recovery uses the max-silence backstop; there is no retransmission timer.}
\label{fig:fault}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{6-DOF transition.} Replacing double-integrator followers with
6-DOF quadrotors degrades every method's accuracy, and leaves the ordering
between methods intact (Figure~\ref{fig:sixdof}). Adaptivity survives the
plant change.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig09_di_vs_6dof}
\caption{Double-integrator against 6-DOF quadrotor followers. Left: every
method lies above the identity line --- all degrade --- while their
ordering is preserved. Right: the adaptive rate response under 6-DOF.}
\label{fig:sixdof}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Plant mismatch and estimator noise.} Both were tested at a
pre-registered combined arm and both rejected an absolute robustness
claim (Figure~\ref{fig:mismatch}). Under combined mismatch --- external
forcing, $+10\%$ true mass, $+20\%$ true drag --- formation error rises
several-fold for \emph{every} method, and the attribution is that the
controller has no integral action, so a mass offset leaves a steady error
no communication policy can remove. Under combined estimator noise and
latency, Clean-condition DATA traffic rises by a factor of
\MetricCleanNoiseRatio{} against the noiseless arm, breaching a
pre-registered bound of $2$: measurement noise itself crosses the
hard-innovation threshold and manufactures triggers.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig10_mismatch_estimator}
\caption{Left: plant-mismatch arms; the mass arm dominates and the effect
is a steady offset. Right: estimator-noise arms; traffic rises past the
pre-registered bound while accuracy does not improve correspondingly ---
the extra transmissions are false triggers.}
\label{fig:mismatch}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Timestep sensitivity.} Formation error is stable across outer
timesteps, but the DATA rate is not: \MetricDtFineData{},
\MetricDtBaseData{} and \MetricDtCoarseData{}~Hz at
$\Delta t = 0.01$, $0.02$ and $0.04$~s. A rate quoted from this campaign
is a rate \emph{at} $\Delta t = 0.02$~s, not a property of the policy.

\subsection{Dataset integrity}

For completeness: across all \MetricRuns{} runs of the final validation
there were \MetricDiverged{} diverged runs and
\MetricInvariants{} protocol-invariant violations. Every realisation hash
matched a registry fixed before the sweep, all \MetricSeeds{} holdout seeds
were used exactly once per method and point, and serial and parallel
execution of the same seed agree bit-identically including trajectories.
