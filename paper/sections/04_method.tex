\section{Method: Causal ACK-Assisted AoI-Aware Triggering}
\label{sec:method}

This section specifies the policy completely enough to reimplement. All
state is per directed channel $(i,j)$; we drop the subscript where it is
unambiguous. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} places the policy in the loop
and Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} shows one round trip including a loss.

\subsection{Sender state: two memories, not one}
\label{sec:dualmem}

The sender maintains, for each channel:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Memory & Governs \\
\midrule
$(\pos^{\mathrm{sent}}, \vel^{\mathrm{sent}})$, $g^{\mathrm{sent}}$, $k^{\mathrm{sent}}$
  & \textbf{innovation} \\
$g^{\mathrm{ack}}$, $k^{\mathrm{ack}}$
  & \textbf{confirmed receiver knowledge} \\
$\mathcal{O}$ (outstanding set)
  & \textbf{in-flight suppression} \\
$t^{\mathrm{tx}}$ (last transmission time)
  & \textbf{all timing gates} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The separation is the load-bearing part of the design, so it is worth
stating why each memory cannot do the other's job.

$(\pos^{\mathrm{sent}}, \vel^{\mathrm{sent}})$ is the last state
\emph{put on the wire}. Innovation must be measured against it, because
the question a trigger answers is ``has the state moved since I last said
anything about it?''. Measuring innovation against the last
\emph{acknowledged} state instead would make the innovation fail to shrink
for a full round trip after every transmission, so the sender would
re-transmit state already in flight. That is not hypothetical: it is
precisely the failure of our first design iteration
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).

$g^{\mathrm{ack}}$ is the generation timestamp of the newest packet an
acknowledgement has \emph{confirmed}. Freshness must be measured against
it, because the question freshness answers is ``how stale is what the
receiver actually has?''. Estimating freshness from $g^{\mathrm{sent}}$
instead --- an open loop --- makes the sender believe the receiver is as
fresh as its own last attempt, which is wrong exactly when packets are
being lost. Measured consequence: the open-loop variant's transmission
rate moves the \emph{wrong way} with network quality, falling from
\MetricSevenAthreecCleanRate{}~Hz to
\MetricSevenAthreecStressedRate{}~Hz per channel as the network degrades.

$\mathcal{O}$ holds the sequence numbers of transmitted-but-unacknowledged
packets. It is a count of what the sender has not heard back about, and it
must not depend on whether the channel dropped a packet --- a real sender
cannot know that. The drop flag exists in our implementation only for
verification counters and never enters a decision.

\subsection{Estimated receiver freshness}

At outer step $t$ with step size $\Delta t$,
\begin{equation}
\Aoihat(t) = t - g^{\mathrm{ack}} + \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t .
\label{eq:estaoi}
\end{equation}
The half-step term is a midpoint correction for the sampling interval and
is kept identical to the convention used for the true-AoI metric
\eqref{eq:trueaoi}, so the estimate and the ground truth remain
comparable.

Equation~\eqref{eq:estaoi} is monotone in wall-clock time between
acknowledgements and jumps down only when an acknowledgement arrives.
Loss and reverse-channel delay therefore inflate $\Aoihat$ relative to the
true $A$, which is the intended behaviour: the sender should become more
willing to transmit precisely when it has stopped hearing back.

An ablation switch replaces $g^{\mathrm{ack}}$ with $g^{\mathrm{sent}}$ to
obtain the open-loop variant discussed above. It is reported as an
ablation, never as the method.

\subsection{Freshness-adaptive threshold scale}

Let $\bar{A}$ be the freshness threshold, $\rho$ the adaptation range,
$s_0$ the base scale and $s_{\min}$ the floor. Define the normalised
staleness excess
\begin{equation}
u(t) =
\begin{cases}
0, & \Aoihat(t) \le \bar{A}, \\[2pt]
\min\!\left(
\dfrac{1}{\rho}\left(\dfrac{\Aoihat(t)}{\bar{A}} - 1\right), \, 1
\right), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:excess}
\end{equation}
and the adaptive scale
\begin{equation}
\scale(t) = s_0 - (s_0 - s_{\min})\, u(t)
\;\in\; [s_{\min},\, s_0] .
\label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}

With the locked values $s_0 = 0.50$, $s_{\min} = 0.20$ and $\rho = 1$: at
$\Aoihat = \bar{A}$ the scale is $0.50$, and at $\Aoihat = 2\bar{A}$ it has
saturated at $0.20$. The saturation is not incidental --- it is what makes
the policy's response to severe reverse-channel impairment flat, which we
return to in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

Dynamic rather than fixed thresholds are established in event-triggered
control~\cite{girard2015dynamic,yi2017distributed}; what differs here is
the modulating signal, which refers to another agent's state of knowledge
rather than to an internal variable.

Innovation magnitudes are measured against the transmitted memory:
\begin{equation}
\Delta p(t) = \| \pos(t) - \pos^{\mathrm{sent}} \|, \qquad
\Delta v(t) = \| \vel(t) - \vel^{\mathrm{sent}} \| .
\label{eq:innov}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The four branches}

The sender transmits when one of four conditions holds. The branches are
ordered, mutually exclusive in effect, and differ in which timing gate
applies to them --- that difference is the contribution.

\paragraph{Global refractory.} No branch may fire within
$\tau_{\min}$ of the previous transmission:
\begin{equation}
t - t^{\mathrm{tx}} < \tau_{\min} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{no transmission}.
\label{eq:refractory}
\end{equation}
This bounds the instantaneous rate and excludes Zeno behaviour.

\paragraph{Branch 1 --- hard new information.}
\begin{equation}
\Delta p \ge \epsP \;\;\vee\;\; \Delta v \ge \epsV .
\label{eq:hard}
\end{equation}
The conventional state-event condition. Subject only to
\eqref{eq:refractory}.

\paragraph{Branch 2 --- freshness-adaptive new information.}
\begin{equation}
\Aoihat \ge \bar{A}
\;\wedge\;
\bigl( \Delta p \ge \scale\,\epsP \;\vee\; \Delta v \ge \scale\,\epsV \bigr).
\label{eq:adaptive}
\end{equation}
The receiver is stale \emph{and} the state has moved past the lowered
threshold. This is new information, so the refresh cooldown does
\textbf{not} apply to it. Note that $\Aoihat \ge \bar{A}$ alone never
transmits: age modulates the threshold, it does not trigger.

\paragraph{Branch 3 --- refresh / retransmission.}
\begin{equation}
\Aoihat \ge \bar{A}
\;\wedge\;
t - t^{\mathrm{tx}} \ge \tau_{r}
\;\wedge\;
|\mathcal{O}| = 0 .
\label{eq:refresh}
\end{equation}
Stale, but with nothing materially new to say. This is the only branch
that repeats information the receiver may already be about to get, so it
is the only branch the cooldown $\tau_r$ governs, and it is additionally
forbidden while any packet is outstanding.

\paragraph{Branch 4 --- max-silence backstop.}
\begin{equation}
t - t^{\mathrm{tx}} \ge \tau_{\max} .
\label{eq:maxsilence}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Loss recovery without a retransmission timer}

The policy introduces no RTO, no round-trip-time estimator and no window
size. Recovery is a consequence of the branch semantics:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A lost packet is never acknowledged, so its sequence number stays in
      $\mathcal{O}$.
\item A non-empty $\mathcal{O}$ blocks Branch 3 by
      \eqref{eq:refresh}.
\item $g^{\mathrm{ack}}$ stays frozen, so $\Aoihat$ grows by
      \eqref{eq:estaoi}, which drives $\scale \to s_{\min}$ by
      \eqref{eq:scale} and makes Branch 2 progressively easier to satisfy.
\item If the state is genuinely static, so that Branch 2 also stays quiet,
      Branch 4 fires at $\tau_{\max}$.
\end{enumerate}

Whether this semantic arrangement alone suffices --- rather than an
explicit retransmission mechanism --- was the question the third design
iteration existed to answer, and it is answered empirically in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Algorithms}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Sender-side causal AoI policy, per channel $(i,j)$, per outer step}
\label{alg:sender}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\pos,\vel$ (local estimate), sender memories
$\pos^{\mathrm{sent}},\vel^{\mathrm{sent}},g^{\mathrm{ack}},
 t^{\mathrm{tx}},\mathcal{O}, k^{\mathrm{sent}}$
\Statex \textbf{Step 1: apply arrived acknowledgements} (Alg.~\ref{alg:receiver}, part B)
\Statex \textbf{Step 2: evaluate the trigger}
\State $\Aoihat \gets t - g^{\mathrm{ack}} + \Delta t/2$
  \Comment{\eqref{eq:estaoi}}
\If{$t - t^{\mathrm{tx}} < \tau_{\min}$}
  \State \Return \textsc{no-send} \Comment{\eqref{eq:refractory}}
\EndIf
\State $\Delta p \gets \|\pos - \pos^{\mathrm{sent}}\|$;\;
       $\Delta v \gets \|\vel - \vel^{\mathrm{sent}}\|$
\State $u \gets \textsc{clamp}\!\left(\frac{1}{\rho}
        \left(\frac{\Aoihat}{\bar A}-1\right),0,1\right)$ if
        $\Aoihat > \bar A$, else $0$
\State $\scale \gets s_0 - (s_0-s_{\min})\,u$
  \Comment{\eqref{eq:scale}}
\State $\textit{stale} \gets (\Aoihat \ge \bar A)$
\If{$\Delta p \ge \epsP$ \textbf{or} $\Delta v \ge \epsV$}
  \State \Return \textsc{send}, branch $\gets$ \textsc{hard}
    \Comment{\eqref{eq:hard}}
\EndIf
\If{\textit{stale} \textbf{and}
    ($\Delta p \ge \scale\,\epsP$ \textbf{or} $\Delta v \ge \scale\,\epsV$)}
  \State \Return \textsc{send}, branch $\gets$ \textsc{adaptive-new}
    \Comment{\eqref{eq:adaptive}}
\EndIf
\If{\textit{stale}}
  \If{$t - t^{\mathrm{tx}} \ge \tau_r$ \textbf{and} $|\mathcal{O}| = 0$}
    \State \Return \textsc{send}, branch $\gets$ \textsc{refresh}
      \Comment{\eqref{eq:refresh}}
  \EndIf
\EndIf
\If{$t - t^{\mathrm{tx}} \ge \tau_{\max}$}
  \State \Return \textsc{send}, branch $\gets$ \textsc{max-silence}
    \Comment{\eqref{eq:maxsilence}}
\EndIf
\State \Return \textsc{no-send}
\Statex \textbf{Step 3: on \textsc{send}}
\State $k^{\mathrm{sent}} \gets k^{\mathrm{sent}} + 1$
\State $\pos^{\mathrm{sent}} \gets \pos$;\;
       $\vel^{\mathrm{sent}} \gets \vel$;\;
       $g^{\mathrm{sent}} \gets t$;\;
       $t^{\mathrm{tx}} \gets t$
\State $\mathcal{O} \gets \mathcal{O} \cup \{k^{\mathrm{sent}}\}$
  \Comment{kept even if the channel drops it}
\State emit packet $\langle k^{\mathrm{sent}}, t, \pos, \vel \rangle$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Receiver DATA acceptance and cumulative ACK}
\label{alg:receiver}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Part A --- receiver, per channel, per tick}
\Require arrived packets $\mathcal{P}$, stored $g_{ij}$, $k^{\mathrm{acc}}_{ij}$
\State sort $\mathcal{P}$ by arrival time, then generation time
\ForAll{$\langle k, g, \pos, \vel\rangle \in \mathcal{P}$}
  \If{$g > g_{ij}$}
    \State store $(\pos,\vel)$;\;
           $g_{ij} \gets g$;\;
           $k^{\mathrm{acc}}_{ij} \gets k$
      \Comment{newest generation wins}
  \Else
    \State \textsc{stale-discard} \Comment{counted, not applied}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$g_{ij}$ advanced during this tick}
  \State emit \textbf{one} ACK $\langle k^{\mathrm{acc}}_{ij}, g_{ij}\rangle$
    \Comment{cumulative; at most one per link per tick}
\EndIf
\Statex
\Statex \textbf{Part B --- sender, applying an arrived ACK}
\Require ACK $\langle k^{a}, g^{a} \rangle$, sender $g^{\mathrm{ack}}, k^{\mathrm{ack}}, \mathcal{O}$
\If{$k^{a} \le k^{\mathrm{ack}}$}
  \State \textsc{stale-ack-discard} \Comment{a late duplicate; not an error}
  \State \Return
\EndIf
\If{$k^{a} \notin \mathcal{O}$}
  \State \textsc{invariant violation: unknown sequence}
\EndIf
\State $g^{\mathrm{ack}} \gets g^{a}$;\;
       $k^{\mathrm{ack}} \gets k^{a}$
\State $\mathcal{O} \gets \{ k \in \mathcal{O} : k > k^{a} \}$
  \Comment{cumulative retirement}
\State \textbf{assert} $g^{\mathrm{ack}}$ non-decreasing
  \Comment{no sender rollback}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Three properties of Algorithm~\ref{alg:receiver} matter for correctness.

\textbf{Cumulative acknowledgement}, as used in ACK-driven AoI
scheduling~\cite{ceran2019average}. One ACK names the newest accepted
packet and retires every outstanding sequence number up to it. This bounds
reverse traffic at one frame per link per tick regardless of how many DATA
packets arrived in that tick, and it makes recovery from a lost ACK
automatic: the next ACK covers everything the lost one would have.

\textbf{Stale-ACK rejection.} An acknowledgement naming a sequence number
already retired is discarded, not applied. Without this, reordering on the
reverse channel could move $g^{\mathrm{ack}}$ backwards, and the sender
would conclude the receiver had become \emph{less} informed --- a
non-physical belief that would inflate $\Aoihat$ and cause spurious
transmission.

\textbf{One-way advance.} $g^{\mathrm{ack}}$ is advanced by exactly one
place in the implementation, the ACK handler. Nothing else may write it.
This is what makes the causality requirement checkable rather than
aspirational.

\subsection{Protocol invariants}

Nine conditions are asserted at runtime, and all nine were zero across all
\MetricRuns{} runs of the final validation:
an ACK never precedes its own accept; no ACK exists for data that never
arrived; $g^{\mathrm{ack}}$ never rolls back; no generation timestamp lies
in the future; no stale ACK is accepted; no ACK names an unknown sequence
number; sequence and generation timestamps agree; the adaptive-new branch
never fires without innovation; and refresh never fires while a useful
packet is in flight.

These are not unit tests of the simulator --- they are properties of the
protocol that would be violated by the most natural implementation
mistakes, in particular by any accidental same-timestep read of receiver
state.

\subsection{What is deliberately absent}

For clarity about the scope of the contribution: the policy has no
retransmission timeout, no round-trip-time estimator, no congestion
window, no negotiated rate, no learned component, and no parameter that
differs between network conditions. The complete parameter set is
Table~\ref{tab:params}, and it is identical in every cell of every
experiment in this paper.
