\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This section explains the mechanisms behind the results, including and
especially the negative ones. Every subsection below corresponds to
something the data shows and the prose could otherwise have glossed over.

\subsection{Why causal feedback helps}

A state-only trigger fires on what the \emph{sender} sees. Its blind spot
is a receiver whose copy has gone stale for a reason invisible locally: a
dropped packet, a long delay, a reordered arrival. In those situations the
sender's state may be perfectly steady, no trigger fires, and the
receiver's error grows unobserved. The measured cost of that blind spot is
large --- the state-event baseline reaches
\MetricRmseEventStressed{}~m in the Stressed condition against
\MetricRmseCausalStressed{}~m for the proposed policy, at
\MetricDataEventStressed{}~Hz against \MetricDataCausalStressed{}~Hz.

Acknowledgements close exactly that loop, and they do so with information
the sender is entitled to have. An ACK is evidence that a specific packet
arrived; the absence of one is evidence that something may not have. The
adaptive threshold turns that evidence into willingness to transmit, and
the direction is right by construction: the longer the silence from the
receiver, the lower the bar for saying something again.

\subsection{Why oracle feedback overstates efficiency}

An acausal AoI policy reads the receiver's true age directly. It is the
natural ideal-feedback reference, and our results show that reading it
as an accuracy bound is a mistake.

The oracle transmits \MetricSevenIdealStressedRate{}~Hz per channel for
\MetricSevenIdealStressedRmse{}~m; the causal policy transmits
\MetricSevenAfivecStressedRate{}~Hz for
\MetricSevenAfivecStressedRmse{}~m. The oracle is \emph{cheaper and less
accurate}. The reason is that perfect freshness knowledge is mostly useful
for deciding when \emph{not} to transmit: an oracle knows immediately when
a packet has landed, so it can go quiet with confidence. A causal sender
cannot distinguish ``delivered, ACK still in flight'' from ``lost'', so it
errs toward transmitting --- and in a lossy network that pessimism is
useful.

The consequence for the literature is specific. An oracle-freshness
baseline bounds the \emph{efficiency} attainable by a causal policy, not
its accuracy, and reporting an oracle's traffic figure as the target a
real implementation should approach understates how much traffic causality
actually costs.

\subsection{Why v1 over-transmits}

The first iteration used a single memory: both innovation and freshness
were measured against the last \emph{acknowledged} state. Under a round
trip the innovation then cannot shrink for at least one round-trip time
(RTT) after every transmission, because the reference point does not move until the ACK
returns. The sender therefore re-transmits state that is already in
flight, hard triggers dominate, and the design breached its
pre-registered resource ceiling.

The lesson generalises past our protocol: innovation must be measured
against what was \emph{said}, not against what was \emph{confirmed}. They
are the same quantity only on a zero-delay channel, which is the case the
design must not be tuned for.

\subsection{Why v2 over-suppresses}

The second iteration fixed that with the dual memory, and introduced the
opposite failure. With in-flight suppression removing most hard triggers,
essentially all traffic flowed through the freshness branch --- and that
branch carried a repetition cooldown. A cooldown intended to limit
\emph{repetition} became a ceiling on \emph{new information}, and the rate
pinned at \MetricSevenAfourcModerateRate{}~Hz in Moderate and
\MetricSevenAfourcStressedRate{}~Hz in Stressed: the same number, meaning
the policy could no longer perceive a difference between the two networks.

This is worth stating as a design pathology rather than a bug. The
parameter was correct, its value was correct, and it was applied to the
wrong set of events.

\subsection{Why v3 resolves the semantic conflict}

The third iteration changes no parameter. It splits the freshness branch in
two according to whether the transmission carries \emph{new information}
or merely \emph{repeats} information:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Traffic & Cooldown $\tau_r$ & In-flight block \\
\midrule
hard new information       & no  & no  \\
adaptive new information   & no  & no  \\
refresh / retransmission   & yes & yes \\
max-silence backstop       & no  & no  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Only the third row can duplicate something the receiver may already be
about to receive, so only the third row is governed by the cooldown and by
in-flight suppression. Once that distinction exists, adaptivity is restored
(\MetricSevenAfivecCleanRate{} $\to$ \MetricSevenAfivecModerateRate{}
$\to$ \MetricSevenAfivecStressedRate{}~Hz) and the error falls below both
the pinned variant and the oracle.

The general point is that ``rate limiting'' is not one concept. Limiting
how often a system may \emph{speak} and limiting how often it may
\emph{repeat itself} are different constraints, and conflating them costs
adaptivity.

\subsection{Why ACK-inclusive cost reverses the Stressed conclusion}

The proposed policy generates one DATA packet per trigger and, on the
reverse path, up to one ACK per link per tick whenever the receiver's
stored state advanced. In a lossy network the receiver's state advances
often relative to the sender's trigger rate, so ACK traffic does not scale
down with DATA traffic --- at the nominal Stressed cell the policy runs
\MetricDataCausalStressed{}~Hz of DATA and
\MetricAckCausalStressed{}~Hz of ACK.

Consequently a DATA-only accounting credits the policy with a saving that
the reverse channel spends. Against P20 the DATA difference is
\MetricKtwoaMean{}~Hz and the ACK-inclusive difference is
\MetricKtwobMean{}~Hz. The periodic baselines have no reverse channel at
all, so pricing acknowledgements moves them not at all and moves our
method to the right by the full weighted ACK rate
(Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}).

We draw the conservative conclusion: the contribution of this work is
adaptivity and accuracy, not a reduction in total radio traffic. Any
deployment argument based on traffic must price the reverse channel, and
under the reference model our method is not the cheapest option in any
cell of the final matrix.

\subsection{Why broadcast accounting favours synchronised periodic traffic}

Under a broadcast medium one transmission from a node reaches all of its
listeners at once. Periodic senders fire on a clock, so their unicast
links collapse almost perfectly into single broadcasts, and their cost
under \eqref{eq:costbcast} falls close to the per-sender rate. An
event-triggered sender fires when \emph{its own} state moves, which is not
synchronised with anything, so its transmissions collapse far less. Add
unicast acknowledgements, which do not collapse at all, and the ordering
changes: Moderate non-dominance falls from
\MetricParetoModerateWtwentyfive{}\% under the reference model to
\MetricParetoModerateBroadcast{}\% under broadcast.

This is a real disadvantage of event-triggered communication on a shared
broadcast medium and we do not argue it away. It also identifies the most
valuable direction for future protocol work: an event-triggered policy
that aligns its transmissions across a sender's outgoing links, or that
aggregates acknowledgements across links, would recover part of this gap.
Our current policy already sends one payload per sender per instant to all
listeners; what it does not do is align \emph{across} senders, and its
acknowledgements remain per link.

\subsection{Why degree-dependent consensus gain creates safety boundaries}

The formation law sums relative terms over a follower's in-neighbours
without normalising by in-degree. The effective loop gain therefore scales
with the number of neighbours, so a denser graph is a
higher-gain system. Beyond a point that produces overshoot and closer
approaches between vehicles --- a safety failure that appears when the
topology gets denser, not when the network gets worse.

This is a controller property. A follow-up diagnostic in the campaign
confirmed it by normalising the gain, which removed both the degree trend
and the safety failure. We report it as a boundary on the campaign's
generalisation and explicitly not as a communication result: none of the
four methods avoids it, and the proposed policy neither causes nor cures
it.

\subsection{Why mass mismatch creates a steady offset}

The position controller is proportional-derivative with a gravity
feedforward computed from the \emph{nominal} mass. If the true mass is
larger, the feedforward under-compensates weight by a constant, and with
no integral action the loop settles at a constant error where the
proportional term makes up the difference. The offset is predominantly
vertical because the mismatch enters through the thrust axis.

The measured effect is a several-fold rise in formation error at the
combined mismatch arm, for every method alike. Calling this a
communication failure would be wrong: no transmission schedule can supply
a term the control law does not contain. The fix is a controller change ---
integral action or explicit disturbance estimation --- and it is outside
the scope of a communication study. We report the rejection and the
attribution together so the reader is not left to guess which layer owns
it.

\subsection{Why measurement noise causes false event triggers}

The hard-innovation branch compares $\|\pos - \pos^{\mathrm{sent}}\|$
against a fixed threshold. With a noisy state estimate, that norm has a
noise floor of order the position noise standard deviation times
$\sqrt{3}$, independent of whether the vehicle moved. When that floor
approaches the threshold, noise alone crosses it and the trigger fires on
measurement error rather than on motion.

The Clean condition is where this hurts most, because there is no channel
impairment to justify the extra traffic: DATA rises by a factor of
\MetricCleanNoiseRatio{} against the noiseless arm, breaching the
pre-registered factor-of-two bound. Accuracy does not improve
correspondingly, which is the signature of false triggering rather than
useful transmission.

The mechanism suggests its own remedy, and we state it as future work
rather than claiming it: the innovation test should be applied to a
filtered estimate, or the threshold should be inflated by a term
proportional to the estimator's own noise level, so that the trigger
responds to motion in excess of measurement uncertainty. We have not
implemented or evaluated either, and the frozen result stands as a
rejection.

\subsection{Why the communication rate depends on the outer timestep}

The trigger is evaluated once per outer control step. The number of
opportunities to transmit in a fixed mission is therefore inversely
proportional to $\Delta t$, and the refractory bound $\tau_{\min}$ is set
to one step in our configuration --- so the maximum attainable rate scales
with $1/\Delta t$ by construction. The measured rates,
\MetricDtFineData{}, \MetricDtBaseData{} and \MetricDtCoarseData{}~Hz at
$\Delta t = 0.01$, $0.02$ and $0.04$~s, follow that.

Formation error, by contrast, is stable across the same range, so the
policy's control behaviour is not $\Delta t$-sensitive in the way its
traffic is. The honest consequence is a reporting rule rather than a fix:
every rate in this paper is a rate at $\Delta t = 0.02$~s. A
$\Delta t$-invariant formulation would need to decouple the trigger
evaluation interval from the control step, or express $\tau_{\min}$ in
physical time independent of the step size. We have not done either.
