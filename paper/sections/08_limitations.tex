\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The campaign was pre-registered, so the boundaries below are recorded
results rather than caveats added afterwards. Each is stated with what
would be needed to remove it.

\subsection{No hardware validation}

This is a simulation study. No claim in this paper has been validated on
hardware, and none of the timing, airtime or energy quantities has been
measured on a radio or a processor. The airtime cost model
\eqref{eq:costair} prices frames by assumed byte counts and the broadcast
model \eqref{eq:costbcast} is an accounting proxy, not a medium-access
simulation. A staged hardware programme --- protocol emulation, embedded
timing, two-node radio, network-in-loop, then multi-vehicle --- is
specified in \texttt{docs/HARDWARE\_ROADMAP.md}, with the simulation
assumption each phase is designed to test.

\subsection{The communication saving is DATA-only}

Under the reference cost model the proposed policy is not the cheapest of
the four methods in any cell of the final matrix
(\MetricCausalCheapestCells{} of \MetricTotalCells{}), and against $20$~Hz
periodic in the Stressed condition its ACK-inclusive cost is higher by
\MetricKtwobMean{}~Hz with an interval excluding zero. Removing this
limitation is a protocol question rather than a tuning question:
acknowledgements would have to be aggregated across links, piggybacked on
data the receiver is already sending, or made less frequent without losing
the freshness signal that makes the policy work.

\subsection{Broadcast accounting is unfavourable}

Under a broadcast medium the Moderate non-dominated fraction falls from
\MetricParetoModerateWtwentyfive{}\% to
\MetricParetoModerateBroadcast{}\%. Synchronised periodic senders collapse
into single broadcasts far more effectively than event-triggered ones, and
unicast acknowledgements do not collapse at all. This is a structural
disadvantage of the approach on a shared medium, not a measurement
artefact.

\subsection{Controller-owned limitations that no policy removes}

Three of the rejected claims belong to the control layer, and we are
explicit that our contribution neither causes nor cures them:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Degree-dependent safety.} The consensus gain is not
      normalised by in-degree, so denser graphs are higher-gain systems.
      A diagnostic confirmed the attribution by normalising the gain,
      which removed both the trend and the safety failure.
\item \textbf{Steady offset under mass mismatch.} The position loop has no
      integral action, so a mass error leaves a constant error that no
      transmission schedule can supply a correction for. Formation error
      rises several-fold at the combined mismatch arm for \emph{every}
      method.
\item \textbf{Shared safety failure under link and node faults.} Under
      $20\%$ permanent link removal and a $5$~s node blackout, the unsafe
      fraction is the same for P10, P20 and the proposed policy. A failure
      identical across a $20$~Hz periodic baseline and an event-triggered
      policy is not a property of either.
\end{itemize}

Fixing any of these requires a controller change --- degree normalisation,
integral action or explicit disturbance estimation --- which is outside the
scope of a communication study and was deliberately not attempted, because
retuning the controller per condition would have measured the quality of
the retuning instead of the robustness of the policy.

\subsection{Estimator and disturbance models are synthetic}

The estimator noise standard deviations, the measurement latency and the
external-force magnitudes are \emph{parameter assumptions}. They are not
measurements from any sensor or airframe, and the estimator sigmas in
particular must not be read as a sensor model. The external forcing is a
world-frame nominal-mass-equivalent acceleration proxy: it does not depend
on relative airspeed, carries no drag coefficient and does not scale with
frontal area, so it is not an aerodynamic wind model.

Within that scope the campaign found a real boundary: at the combined
estimator arm, Clean-condition traffic rises by a factor of
\MetricCleanNoiseRatio{} because measurement noise itself crosses the
hard-innovation threshold. A noise-aware trigger --- filtering before the
innovation test, or inflating the threshold by the estimator's own noise
level --- is the obvious remedy and is \emph{not} evaluated here.

\subsection{Rates are quoted at one timestep}

Formation error is stable across outer timesteps but the transmission rate
is not: \MetricDtFineData{}, \MetricDtBaseData{} and
\MetricDtCoarseData{}~Hz at $\Delta t = 0.01$, $0.02$ and $0.04$~s. Every
rate in this paper is therefore a rate \emph{at} $\Delta t = 0.02$~s. A
$\Delta t$-invariant formulation would need to decouple trigger evaluation
from the control step, which we have not done.

\subsection{Scope of the final validation}

The holdout validation ran one selected point per robustness axis, on
ring topology only, at $\Delta t = 0.02$~s, and did not run the estimator
point in the Clean condition. It therefore carries \emph{no evidence}
about other topologies, other timesteps, or Clean-condition estimator
noise, and the corresponding earlier rejections stand untouched rather
than being narrowed. One point cannot retract a sweep.

\subsection{Leader model and swarm scale}

The leader is a kinematic reference in the 6-DOF study: it follows its
trajectory exactly and is not itself a dynamic vehicle. It is also never
selected for a communication blackout, since removing the formation's only
absolute reference is a different experiment. The largest swarm studied is
$N=50$ with double-integrator followers; the 6-DOF results are at $N=5$,
with a secondary characterisation at $N=10$. No result here establishes
6-DOF behaviour at large $N$.

\subsection{Single formation geometry and mission}

All results use one formation lattice, one leader trajectory and one
mission duration. Formation geometry enters several of the safety results
directly --- the minimum-separation threshold is a property of the lattice
spacing --- so safety numbers should not be transferred to a different
geometry.
