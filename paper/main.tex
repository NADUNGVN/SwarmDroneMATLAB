\begin{abstract}
Fixed periodic schedules cannot adjust effort inside a changing mission, while
a sender cannot directly observe receiver freshness across delayed or lossy
DATA and ACK channels. We present a fixed causal ACK-assisted,
age-of-information-aware policy that
adapts communication effort as network quality changes without receiving a
regime label. Separate last-transmitted and ACK-confirmed memories prevent
in-flight packets from masquerading as receiver knowledge, while distinct
new-information and refresh branches suppress repetition without suppressing
innovation. In a preregistered 50-seed holdout, the Stressed paired
RMSE difference (Causal-v3$-$P10) is \MetricKoneMean{} m (95\% CI
$[\MetricKoneLo{},\MetricKoneHi{}]$). The communication conclusion depends on
accounting: relative to P20, the signed Causal-v3$-$P20 differences are
\MetricKtwoaMean{} Hz for DATA and \MetricKtwobMean{} Hz for
DATA$+0.25$ACK. In a separate changing-network
mission, the same fixed policy changes rate at every adjacent regime transition
and has lower ACK-weighted cost than P20 at preregistered $w=0.25$. Yet P12.5
retains \MetricElevenPonetwofiveAccuracyPercent{}\% of Causal-v3 accuracy at
\MetricElevenPonetwofiveCostPercent{}\% of Causal-v3's cost, and broadcast accounting
reverses the P20 comparison. The evidence supports within-mission adaptivity,
not universal dominance or general traffic reduction. Validation is
simulation-only, including 6-DOF followers; no hardware or stability claim is
made.
\end{abstract}
