\begin{abstract}
Fixed-rate inter-agent communication cannot adapt to a network whose
quality is unknown or changing, while a conventional state-event trigger
saves traffic but ignores how stale the receiver's copy has become. We
present a causal ACK-assisted, age-of-information-aware event-triggered
communication policy for multi-UAV formation control. Each sender
estimates its receiver's freshness \emph{only} from cumulative
acknowledgements crossing a delayed and lossy reverse channel, never by
reading receiver state, and holds that estimate in a memory separate from
the last state it transmitted, so that a repetition cooldown governs
repetition alone rather than genuinely new information. A single fixed
parameterization is used throughout.

On a pre-registered validation over \MetricSeeds{} holdout seeds and
\MetricRuns{} runs with 6-DOF quadrotor followers, the policy lowers
formation error against a $10$~Hz periodic baseline in the most impaired
condition by \MetricKoneMean{}~m (95\% CI
$[\MetricKoneLo{},\MetricKoneHi{}]$), attains lower error than a
conventional state-event trigger in all \MetricTotalCells{} cells of the
final matrix, and adapts its DATA traffic monotonically with network
quality (\MetricDataCausalClean{}, \MetricDataCausalModerate{} and
\MetricDataCausalStressed{}~Hz). The reduction is \emph{DATA-only}:
against $20$~Hz periodic, DATA falls by \MetricKtwoaMean{}~Hz while the
ACK-inclusive total rises by \MetricKtwobMean{}~Hz, both intervals
excluding zero, so no reduction in total radio traffic is claimed.
Results are simulation only; no hardware validation is claimed.
\end{abstract}
