\begin{abstract}
A sender in a networked UAV formation cannot infer a receiver's information
age from its own transmissions when DATA and acknowledgements are delayed,
lost, queued, and contended on a shared medium. We study receiver-confirmed
semantic triggering with broadcast DATA, cumulative feedback, finite queues,
multi-slot contention, and burst losses. The protocol maintains distinct
transmitted and causally confirmed memories; the analysis proves conservative
sender-side age, connects age to semantic state error and formation input-to-
state stability, and derives an exact marginal-airtime identity for standalone
ACKs. The experiments deliberately retain negative results. A fixed initial
design is dominated by a delayed-belief baseline in all primary regimes and
collapses at 20 agents under unscaled access. Access scaling repairs that
failure, but state-cloned focal-decision replay shows that earlier confirmation
can worsen receiver age or formation loss by suppressing later DATA. This
motivates a preregistered MAC-conditioned route: piggyback-only under CSMA and
adaptive standalone feedback under ALOHA. Directional validation supports all
six registered RMSE/load hypotheses. A second frozen matched $2\times2$
holdout then tests the route-by-access interaction within each of 100 new
seeds. All six Holm-adjusted simple-effect/interaction tests pass; the RMSE
interaction is $-0.00927$~m and the offered-airtime interaction is $-0.05296$,
with both 95\% intervals entirely negative. A third frozen holdout expands to
eight load/loss/size contexts; only 11/48 globally adjusted tests and 1/8 full
context rules pass. This rejects configured MAC type as a general selector and
triggers a preregistered stop before hardware. At 20 agents, eligible
standalone decisions occur in only 11 of 300
development trajectories, so no per-decision ACK-value or equivalence claim
is made. The results expose a bounded causal feedback-mediation effect within
the declared abstract model, not universal ACK utility, named-MAC behavior, or
hardware readiness.
\end{abstract}
