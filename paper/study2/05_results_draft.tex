\section{Results}
\label{sec:s2-results}

\subsection{The initial shared-medium design is not the final result}

delayed-ACK belief frontier point in Clean, Moderate, and Stressed. The
negative result is not removed by later development. Table~\ref{tab:s2-initial}
shows the principal mechanism contrasts.

Broadcast aggregation is valuable: hybrid broadcast reduces offered load by
59.05\% and RMSE by 3.50\% relative to causal unicast. That does not validate
standalone feedback. Piggyback-only improves all three continuous outcomes
over hybrid in Moderate. Clean also exposes a control/resource trade-off:
default uses less airtime than P10 but has worse RMSE and true AoI. In
Stressed, the signs reverse. Thus no regime-independent dominance statement is
available.

The strongest boundary is $N=20$. With fixed $p=0.2$, hybrid demand reaches
2.849 channel-times and fails safety in 100/100 runs. Periodic P10 fails only
5/100 at offered utilization 0.484 and lower mean RMSE. The failure is an
access/contention collapse, not evidence that causal freshness alone scales.

\subsection{Factorial repair and selected-policy validation}

EXP14C/D isolate adaptive feedback, the proposed load guard, and access
scaling. Scaling $p$ to $\min\{p_{\rm configured},1/N\}$ is the dominant N20
repair. The fixed load guard lowers offered demand but worsens N20 RMSE by
22.45\%, true AoI by 23.43\%, and causes 6/12 failures versus 0/12 without the
guard. The selected arm is therefore adaptive feedback, scaled access, and no
fixed guard.

EXP14E freezes that arm against access-only hybrid feedback on 100 new seeds.
All six Holm-adjusted primary hypotheses pass:

This validates adaptation relative to the earlier hybrid implementation, not

standalone ACK attempts per run and the continuous differences are unresolved.
Under the mandatory ALOHA boundary, selected instead improves RMSE and load.

\subsection{Confirmation lead is not receiver value}

EXP14F finds a positive generation-matched policy lead for 83.67\%, 78.53\%,
and 96.53\% of matched N5 CSMA, N10, and ALOHA events. Yet the full-policy
sign favors piggyback at N5/N10 and ACK assistance at ALOHA. Because the two
policies evolve independently from mission start, this lead is not the effect
of one focal decision.

EXP14G closes that gap by state-cloned replay. Table~\ref{tab:s2-branch}
reports supported cells; positive benefit means suppress-branch minus
admit-branch. Intervals are descriptive over 20 independent parent decisions.

At N5, earlier confirmation significantly worsens the local true-AoI
integral. At N10, it significantly worsens integrated formation loss. These
are not contradictions: the ACK advances sender belief but carries no state,
and that belief can suppress or reshape later DATA. ALOHA has favorable mean
signs but heterogeneous intervals containing zero. Only 1/20 N5 and 3/20 N10
decisions are Pareto beneficial across resource, true-AoI, and control signs;
ALOHA has 8/20.

N20 fails the frozen support minimum. EXP14G supplies four eligible parent
seeds from 60 pilots, and EXP14H supplies seven from 240. Across the disjoint
blocks, only 11/300 trajectories contain a post-transient candidate with the
complete local window: 3.67\%, with 95\% Wilson interval 2.06--6.45\%.
EXP14H correctly stops before outcome replay. This is a practical event-
support failure, not an ACK-effect estimate.

not constitute population safety evidence.

\subsection{Formal feedback-route-by-MAC interaction}

EXP14I's separate directional tests validate the registered choices but do not
alone test an interaction. EXP16 therefore freezes a matched $2\times2$ design
over MAC (CSMA/ALOHA) and route (adaptive/piggyback) on 100 additional seeds.
All four primary cells in a seed consume the same absolute random arrays and
channel-state realization. A fixed-deadline hybrid route is retained as a
secondary reference. The 600-run matrix passes 14/14 integrity gates with zero
divergence and protocol violations.

For outcome $Y$, define
$D_m(Y)=Y_{\mathrm{adaptive},m}-Y_{\mathrm{piggyback},m}$ and
$I(Y)=D_{\mathrm{ALOHA}}(Y)-D_{\mathrm{CSMA}}(Y)$. Table~\ref{tab:s2-exp16}
reports the frozen six-test family; simple effects are oriented below zero in
their registered favorable direction.

are unresolved under ALOHA, so the result identifies the value of retaining a
standalone path relative to piggyback-only, not superiority of the adaptive
scheduler over every ACK-aware implementation.

\subsection{The operating envelope rejects a general MAC-only selector}

EXP17 freezes eight core contexts spanning $N\in\{5,10\}$,
Moderate/Stressed burst loss, and background load in $\{0,0.30\}$. Each context
retains the same matched $2\times2$ MAC-by-route design on 100 new seeds. All
48 simple-effect/interaction tests share one Holm family. Reverse-asymmetric
and hidden-terminal contexts are descriptive boundaries. The complete
4,000-run matrix passes 14/14 integrity gates with no divergence or protocol
violation.

Table~\ref{tab:s2-exp17} reports the two interaction estimates. A context is
supported only when its two simple effects and interaction pass for both
outcomes and both observed safety guards pass.

Only 11/48 tests reject and only the original base cell satisfies the full
rule, far below the frozen continuation threshold of 6/8. Background 0.30
nearly eliminates both N5 Moderate interactions. At N10 Moderate bg0, CSMA
still favors piggyback, but ALOHA does not favor adaptive on RMSE. Several
high-load ALOHA/N10 cells have 83--100 observed safety failures per route; the
N10 Stressed bg0.30 ALOHA guard also fails by one event.

Reverse asymmetry further separates objectives: adaptive improves ALOHA RMSE
but worsens offered utilization, while the CSMA RMSE simple effect reverses.
The hidden-terminal interactions remain negative, but their CSMA simple
effects are unresolved. EXP17 therefore preserves EXP16 as a base-cell result
and rejects its extension into a general configured-MAC selector. The frozen
hardware-continuation gate is not met.
